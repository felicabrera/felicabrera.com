Over the weekend of the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2025 I presented HEA, my habitable exoplanet analyzer, and it placed first nationally in Uruguay. I competed on my own. Here is the short version.

## What Space Apps is

The NASA International Space Apps Challenge is NASA's hackathon: one weekend, the agency's open data, and teams from all over the world working on a set of challenges. In 2025 people from more than 180 countries took part.

In Uruguay the edition was organized by Space Uruguay and had real volume: 75 projects, more than 100 teams and more than 230 people. From there, five national winners were chosen along with a set of ten projects that advance to the international judging.

## The project

HEA (Habitable Exoplanet Analyzer) uses NASA open data to detect exoplanets with AI and estimate their habitability by comparing them to Earth. The key part: it does not just return a number, it also shows which factors drive each prediction. I wrote up how I built it in [this post](/blog/hea-how-we-built-it).

I competed individually, in the adults ("Mayores") category, as one of the three national winners in it. As I told San Jose Ahora, I got into this as "a form of fun and of applying what I learned at university", and I like to think that sharing the result can inspire other young people from San Jose to explore science, technology and innovation.

## What comes next

Space Apps has a global round after the national one. The 2025 schedule:

- November 26, 2025: international finalists announced.
- December 18, 2025: global winning teams published.

Placing in the national top, and having NASA people evaluate it, is already more than I expected when I started this as a hobby.
