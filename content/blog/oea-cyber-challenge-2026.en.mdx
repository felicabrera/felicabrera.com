With the team **Never be Frog** we took third place nationally in the OEA Cyber Challenge 2026, alongside Stefano Francolino. A short recap.

## What the OEA Cyber Challenge is

It is a cybersecurity challenge organized by the Organization of American States (OAS) together with Agesic. It is free, virtual, and runs on the HackRocks platform. The 2026 edition, the second in Uruguay, ran on October 16 and 17, 2026, drew 69 people aged 18 to 25, and featured six challenges based on real-world scenarios.

## What a CTF is (and why I like them)

CTF stands for "capture the flag": competitions where you solve security challenges and, for each one, find a "flag", a secret string that proves you solved it. Challenges are usually grouped into categories: web, cryptography, forensics, reversing and **pwn** (binary exploitation), among others.

My favorite category is pwn: take a program, find a memory bug (an overflow, a format string, a use-after-free) and chain it until you take control of execution. It is the part of security that feels most like solving a puzzle with very strict rules.

## Why they matter

CTFs are not just sport. They force you to understand how systems actually fail, not in theory: what a corrupted stack looks like, what a memory leak lets you do, why one mitigation stops an attack and another does not. That intuition is the same one you need to defend or audit real systems.

## The result

The top three teams tied at 545 points, so the order came down to solve time:

- **First place: l1ghtn1ng** (Isabela Gómez).
- **Second place: Datasec** (Erik Puglia, Tadeo Goday and Renzo Ramagli).
- **Third place: Never be Frog** (Felipe Cabrera and Stefano Francolino).

Of the six challenges, I wrote up two pwn ones: [VOVKA](/blog/vovka-hackrocks-pwn), with a format string, a stack overflow and ROP, and [IO_HO_FIRM 2.0](/blog/ioho-firm-pagejack), a NULL off-by-one in the kernel.
